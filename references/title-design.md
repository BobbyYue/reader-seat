# Title Design

Use this module for a document title, article headline, subtitle, or title diagnosis. A title is the document's smallest factual promise: it should help the intended reader identify the subject and predict the value of reading without exaggerating what the source proves.

## Core Construction

Prefer this underlying relationship:

`concrete anchor + strongest source-supported relationship, change, decision, or question`

This is a thinking model, not a fixed sentence template.

- The **concrete anchor** identifies the person, event, project, product, metric, decision, source material, or bounded time period.
- The **core relationship** states what the document explains: how choices connect, why a tradeoff exists, what changed, which decision is needed, or what the evidence supports.
- The **reader value** should be inferable from the relationship. Do not append a generic promise such as “一文看懂”.
- Use a colon only when the anchor and relationship form two natural parts. Omit it when one direct sentence reads better.
- When the source contains a concrete actor and a central verified result, do not replace them with a generic thesis merely because the thesis sounds punchier. A specific supported fact usually carries more reader value than a rhetorical contrast.

Example:

`梁文锋四小时发言：DeepSeek 如何围绕 AGI 做取舍`

- anchor: `梁文锋四小时发言`;
- subject: `DeepSeek`;
- core relationship: `围绕 AGI 做取舍`;
- boundary: `如何` introduces an explanatory organization without claiming hidden intent or independent proof that every choice succeeds.

## Candidate Selection

Do not publish the first title that is accurate enough. Generate at least three internal candidates before selecting the visible title; do not show the shortlist unless the user asks.

- **Result or evidence led**: foreground the most decision-useful verified result, change, or event.
- **Mechanism led**: foreground the relationship or operating method the document explains.
- **Problem or decision led**: foreground the concrete problem, tradeoff, or decision the reader must understand.

Reject unsupported candidates first. Compare the remaining candidates in this order:

1. **Information value**: which candidate tells the reader the most useful source-supported information before the body is opened?
2. **Object clarity**: can the reader identify the actual actor, project, product, metric, event, or bounded source?
3. **Document fit**: does it represent the document's main reader value rather than one memorable sentence?
4. **Discrimination**: could the same title be moved unchanged to many unrelated documents? If yes, it is too generic.
5. **Naturalness**: does it sound like a person naming this specific document rather than a reusable headline formula?

When a verified number is central to the document and understandable with its object and scope, test at least one candidate that includes it. Use the number only when it remains accurate without hiding a material qualifier. A title and subtitle may divide the work: put the concrete actor and result in the title, then put the mechanism, scope, or evidence boundary in the subtitle.

Treat `真正`, `背后`, `不是……而是……`, and similar suspense or contrast devices as review signals. Keep them only when the contrast itself is the source-supported central relationship and does not displace a more informative actor, result, event, or decision.

## Mandatory Decision Flow

1. **Identify the reader task**: determine whether the title must help the reader recognize an event, understand a relationship, judge a result, choose an option, or complete a task.
2. **Inventory title material**: list the concrete object, most valuable verified result or relationship, necessary scope, and evidence boundary before writing a title.
3. **Generate candidates**: create at least one result-led, mechanism-led, and problem- or decision-led candidate when the source supports those angles.
4. **Reject factual failures**: remove any candidate that changes scope, certainty, causality, magnitude, attribution, or source status.
5. **Select by information value**: use the comparison in Candidate Selection; do not choose primarily for surprise, rhythm, or rhetorical force.
6. **Calibrate the verb**: distinguish `显示`, `同时发生`, `可能相关`, `解释`, `主张`, `计划`, `决定`, and `导致`. Use only the strongest wording the evidence supports.
7. **Compose naturally**: choose one-part or two-part syntax based on rhythm and clarity. Do not force a colon, number, question, or formula.
8. **Assign subtitle work**: use a subtitle only for scope, audience, time, method, or a necessary evidence boundary that would overload the title.
9. **Verify against the body**: every material implication in the title must be supported, and the opening must promptly fulfill the title's promise.

## Scenario Guidance

| Scenario | Useful title focus | Example pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| News or industry brief | verified actor or event + material development or significance | `对象：发生了什么，以及为什么值得关注` |
| Technical proposal | system or decision + problem, choice, or tradeoff | `系统改造：为什么选择方案 A，以及如何回滚` |
| Product document | product or user + problem, value, or boundary | `产品能力：为哪类用户解决什么问题` |
| Business update | project or period + result, impact, risk, or decision | `项目阶段复盘：完成了什么，还需要决定什么` |
| Data analysis | metric or population + observed change and calibrated interpretation | `留存变化：下降与版本发布同期发生，原因仍待验证` |
| Procedure | task + successful outcome or important condition | `配置访问权限：从准备到验证成功` |
| Interview or source summary | person or source + the central relationship among ideas or choices | `人物发言：某个核心目标如何影响一组选择` |

These are relationship examples, not phrases to copy. Use the source's language and the audience's vocabulary.

## Evidence And Tone Boundaries

- Do not claim hidden intention with words such as `真正想说`, `本质上`, or `其实` unless the source explicitly establishes the contrast.
- Treat `首次`, `唯一`, `全面`, `彻底`, `颠覆`, `终结`, `必然`, `证明`, and similar absolute or promotional words as review signals, not as automatic improvements.
- Do not use a question mark to imply a disputed premise is true.
- Do not turn correlation into causality or a forecast into an event.
- Do not make the title more certain than the body, source, or available verification.
- Natural curiosity may come from a real tension, choice, consequence, or unresolved question. Do not manufacture an information gap the document cannot close.

## Prohibited Outputs

Deceptive or clickbait titles are a hard failure, not an optional style warning. Do not output them, even when the user asks for a more clickable version. Offer the strongest truthful and relevant alternative instead.

Reject or rewrite a title when it does any of the following to obtain attention:

- states a false fact or a claim not supported by the body and source;
- inflates magnitude, certainty, novelty, causality, urgency, conflict, or consequence;
- inserts an unrelated celebrity, company, trend, keyword, or controversy;
- implies a secret, scandal, reversal, threat, or surprising answer the document does not establish;
- asks a leading question that embeds an unsupported premise;
- removes a material qualifier, population, time range, comparison basis, or uncertainty and thereby changes the reader's likely interpretation;
- generalizes one case, anecdote, or subgroup into a broader conclusion;
- promises an answer, method, result, list, or evidence that the body does not deliver promptly;
- presents speculation, rumor, prediction, or third-party interpretation as confirmed or official.

Strong or vivid wording is allowed when it is relevant, precisely defined, source-supported, and proportionate to the event. Do not mechanically weaken a verified major result merely because the title is attention-worthy.

For every candidate title, create an internal title-to-body check: list each material implication in the title and locate the body passage and source that supports it. If any implication lacks support or requires a missing qualifier, revise before delivery.

## Title And Subtitle

- The title carries the subject and central promise.
- The subtitle may carry audience, scope, time, evidence status, or format when necessary.
- Do not repeat the same nouns and conclusion in both.
- Do not put a disclaimer in the subtitle when a more accurate title verb solves the problem.
- For a short document, omit the subtitle unless it changes interpretation.

## Preserve Or Rewrite

- Preserve a supplied title when it is accurate, specific, natural, and still matches the revised document.
- Make a local edit when one word overstates certainty, hides the object, or sounds templated.
- Rewrite the title when the document's primary reader task or central relationship has changed.
- When the user explicitly asks for alternatives, vary the angle or level of directness without changing facts or certainty.

## Final Verification

Before delivery, confirm:

1. the intended reader can identify the document's object without reading the body;
2. the title communicates one central relationship, change, decision, or useful question;
3. every title implication is supported by the body and source;
4. certainty, causality, intent, time, and scope are calibrated;
5. the title is specific enough that it could not be moved unchanged to an unrelated document;
6. the wording sounds natural for the channel and audience rather than promotional, SEO-like, or generated from a template;
7. the opening fulfills the title's promise promptly;
8. the subtitle adds distinct information or is omitted.
9. every material implication maps to a supporting body passage and source;
10. the title contains no false, exaggerated, irrelevant, or unfulfilled promise intended to induce a click.
11. no concrete actor, central verified result, or decision-useful fact was discarded in favor of a lower-information rhetorical hook.
12. the selected title beat the other viable candidates on information value, object clarity, document fit, discrimination, and naturalness.
