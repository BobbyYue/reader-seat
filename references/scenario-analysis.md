# Data Analysis And Research Report

## Reader Task

Enable the reader to understand what changed or differs, assess the evidence and definitions, distinguish explanation from causality, and choose an evidence-proportional next step.

## Required Content

- analytical question, object, population, and decision context;
- main finding with direction, magnitude, comparison basis, and period;
- strongest supporting evidence and material counterevidence;
- metric definitions, source, grain, filters, denominator, sample, timezone, freshness, and method as applicable;
- explanation, alternative explanations, and causal boundary;
- uncertainty, limitations, missing data, and generalizability;
- implication or recommendation no stronger than the evidence.
- when a material conclusion depends on named evidence gaps, a next-validation
  direction that directly addresses those gaps; do not invent an owner,
  deadline, experiment design, or implementation detail that was not supplied.
  This remains required in a concise title-and-conclusion rewrite: use one short
  final sentence unless the user explicitly requests description only and no
  next step.

## Recommended Relationship

`main finding -> strongest evidence -> definitions and method needed for trust -> explanation and alternatives -> implication, limitation, and next validation`

Use “object + change or impact + key evidence + meaning” for an analytical conclusion when all elements are supported. Move reproducibility detail to a method section or appendix without hiding decision-critical definitions.

## Evidence Requirements

- Keep every material claim traceable to data or a cited source.
- State whether a result is descriptive, correlational, quasi-experimental, experimental, or causal.
- Preserve denominator, sample, inclusion and exclusion rules, significance, confidence interval, and multiple-testing context when present.
- Show relevant distribution or segmentation when an average can hide meaningful differences.

## Common Failures

- correlation presented as causation;
- missing denominator, comparison basis, period, unit, or metric definition;
- selectively reported evidence or segments;
- precision stronger than data quality or sample size;
- charts that encode drama instead of the analytical question;
- recommendation that exceeds the observed evidence.

## Acceptance Questions

1. Can the reader restate object, direction, magnitude, baseline, and period?
2. Can each material conclusion be matched to evidence?
3. Can the reader distinguish observation, explanation, causal claim, and unknown?
4. Are metric definition, source, grain, filters, denominator, and freshness adequate?
5. Does the recommendation stay within the evidence boundary?
6. When evidence gaps block the judgment, does the next validation address the
   named gap without adding unsupported execution detail?
