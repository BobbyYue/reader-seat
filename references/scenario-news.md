# News And Industry Brief

## Reader Task

Help the reader identify what happened, how it is known, why it matters now, and what remains uncertain.

## Required Content

- verified development, affected object, and event time;
- source attribution and publication or update time;
- context needed to interpret significance;
- observed or likely impact, with interpretation clearly separated from fact;
- material unknowns, disputes, and what would confirm the next update.
- when visuals are used, documentary images from the source or independently verified assets, with conceptual or generated alternatives clearly distinguished.

## Recommended Relationship

`verified development and time -> source and context -> significance or impact -> unknowns and next update`

Use chronology when sequence changes meaning. Use topic grouping when comparing several related developments. Do not add recommendations unless the brief's explicit purpose includes analysis or action.

## Evidence Requirements

- Prefer primary records, named sources, and direct official statements.
- Attribute claims close to the sentence they support.
- Distinguish event time, publication time, and document update time.
- Label inference, forecast, commentary, and disputed information.
- Verify AI-generated or secondary-source factual claims before publication.
- Treat a real-person or event image as a factual identity and context claim. Follow [visual-evidence.md](visual-evidence.md) and reject assets supported only by search metadata or aggregator captions.

## Common Failures

- rumor, forecast, or interpretation presented as established fact;
- unattributed claims or vague “reports say” phrasing;
- old information presented as a new development;
- headline stronger than the body or source;
- fact and opinion mixed in one sentence;
- false balance that gives unsupported claims equal weight.
- an unrelated, wrong-context, or unverified photo presented as evidence of the named person or event;
- a generated illustration presented or captioned like a real photo.

## Acceptance Questions

1. Can the reader state the verified development and its time?
2. Can they identify who supports each material claim?
3. Can they separate fact, quotation, interpretation, and unknown?
4. Can they explain why the update matters without inventing impact?
5. Is the document clear about what may change next?
6. Can the reader distinguish documentary visuals from contextual or generated visuals, and trace every documentary identity claim?
